Study summary

The study develops dedicated corridors for six- and twelve-vertiport networks in the São Paulo Metropolitan Region using a reproducible workflow and supporting 2D/3D dashboard. Nominal corridors are 0.5 NM wide and 1,000 ft high, centered at 4,000 ft MSL. Selected waypoints are displaced laterally and lowered to 3,000 ft to reduce geometric interference with existing airspace structures.

Volumetric analysis indicates that refinement lowers the total intersection share from 23.7% to 18.2%, primarily due to a reduction in REA overlap. The radial Congonhas (SBSP) flow envelope nevertheless contains 14.0% of refined corridor volume; the more localized clustered-stream envelope yields 2.0%. These findings motivate joint evaluation of volumetric intersection and observed flight-track exposure.

Method in brief

Vertiports and routes

Six and twelve vertiports plus the three airports are connected using shortest paths on the REH graph. Demand uses Metrô OD and ANAC airport passenger inputs.

Geometry

Two opposite-direction lanes, nominally 0.50 NM in total width and 1,000 ft high. In identified conflict regions, corridors are displaced and lowered.

Empirical verification

December 2022 Flightradar24 tracks support radial and clustered-stream airport-flow envelopes at SBSP and SBGR.

Dashboard workflow

Analyst-selected adjustments are checked visually in 2D/3D and quantified by automated 50-m-cell volumetric overlap analysis.

Why empirical flow representation matters

The same refined network intersects 14.0% of the radial SBSP envelope by corridor volume, but only 2.0% of its clustered-stream representation. Small stream-envelope volume does not necessarily mean low operational exposure: a narrow approach path can carry many movements.

The six-vertiport refinement altered 16 distinct waypoints (220 of 930 point records) and increased total track length by just 0.11%.

Interactive UAM Airspace Dashboard

The SIGMA-Sky UAM Airspace Dashboard is the supporting tool described in the paper. It provides an interactive 2D map and 3D visualization, CSV import/export, route/waypoint editing, parallel lane generation and volume-overlap analysis. The tool supports analyst decisions; it does not automatically optimize corridor placements.

Interpretation

A geometrically plausible UAM network can still intersect historical conventional traffic paths. Geometric overlap does not imply simultaneous loss of aircraft separation or demonstrate that the configuration is operationally safe.

The REH-reference comparison also changes corridor width and lateral geometry. It is not a pure altitude-only controlled experiment.

Limitations and next steps

Limitations include reduced vertical spacing, route-band approximations, mean terrain, RNP-based widths without containment analysis and one month of historical flight records.

A hybrid corridor design near SBSP will be evaluated, with future BlueSky simulations addressing demand–capacity balancing and deconfliction.
